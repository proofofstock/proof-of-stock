# Changelog

All notable changes to Proof of Stock. Format: [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).
Versioning: [SemVer](https://semver.org/).

- **Major:** a breaking change to an answer's shape: a field removed or renamed, a unit changed, an enum value removed,
  or a schema in `schemas/v2/` or `docs/reference/passport.schema.json` changed incompatibly.
- **Minor:** new fields, tools, routes or pages.
- **Patch:** fixes that keep every shape.

The version is in `package.json`, and the site footer, the MCP `serverInfo.version` and the header of every docs page read it from there. No page writes it into its own text: a number written in two places is a number that goes wrong in one of them.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Pool depth on every passport.** `latest.liquidity` gives the size of the deepest eligible pool in US dollars and `thin: true` below 50,000 USD, where a trade of a few thousand dollars moves the price by several percent. The site shows it under the on-chain price, marked "thin liquidity" when it is. It is the pool's size, not a quote, and the issuer's reserves are still not something we can check (owner, 26.11).

### Fixed
- **A one-word company name is an impostor claim only when the token's symbol backs it.** Names like `Snap`, `Tron`, `Unity` or `McDonald's` are also ordinary words and memes: a token called `Snap` with the symbol `THANOS` is not posing as Snap Inc. The symbol must be the ticker or the company name, or contain the ticker (three characters or more) or the name (four or more). Names of two words or more are unchanged. Cases the narrower rule no longer holds close with the reason "our name rule was narrowed", never as if the token had changed (case rule `impostor-strong-v3`; outside review, 26.11).
- **`@proofofstock/guard` 0.3.1: `failOpen` never buys a record the server holds because the chain contradicts it.** A `503` whose `reason` is `factory_conflict` or `issuer_prefix_mismatch` is an answer, not an outage, so it is refused whatever `failOpen` says. Any other `503`, an outage or a listing only awaiting confirmation, is decided by `failOpen` as before, and the reason is named in the decision (outside review, 25.11).

### Changed
- **Demo Agent: Apple and Amazon buttons, quick amounts and a clear word.** Under the field, $10, $100, $1,000 and $10,000 write "stock amount$" into it, the way a reader types it; with no stock there, a random one of the buttons is picked. "clear" empties the field.
- **A record on the issuer's list is called listed only when the chain backs it** (`docs/list-locks-tz.md` v3; the owner accepted the delay for new listings). If an issuer's API list were tampered with, a foreign contract on it would otherwise be called the issuer's own on every route.
  - **What it checks.** On Base, the address must have the pinned prefix `0xb200000000000000000000`. On both chains, the issuer's factory must have created it, as recorded by the finalized-block mirror and, where there is one, confirmed by a second RPC.
  - **What an untrusted record gets.** No verdict: `503` with a `reason` (`issuer_prefix_mismatch`, `factory_check_unavailable`, `factory_conflict`, `awaiting_factory_confirmation`). It is never `listed: true` and never `listed: false`. Its passport is a `503` too.
  - **Where it holds.** One source of listed records feeds every route that says "listed". A batch with a held record fails closed. A held original is never named as the token to buy instead of a look-alike. `list_proof` and the `/api/list` archive still record the issuer's list as served.
  - **Cost.** A genuine new listing turns "listed" some 35 to 50 minutes after its creation.
  - **Status and alerts.** `/api/status` shows the factory check and the held records. `stp-health` alerts `list-conflict` and `factory-check`, and `list-awaiting` when a record has waited over two hours.
  - **After outside review.**
    - One freshness rule serves the list and the verdict: at the 15-minute edge a held record was answered as listed.
    - A stale list is never replaced by the raw one.
    - A copy of a held new listing still resembles it in its passport, without naming the original.
    - `find` names held matches with their reason and no verdict.
    - The archive never quotes a record the chain contradicts now as LISTED.
    - The search box shows a hold instead of another chain's "not on the list".
    - The MCP batch names the held address and why.
    - An address held on another chain says so.
- **The issuer factory mirror is now a source a verdict could rest on** (the first part of `docs/list-locks-tz.md`, after two outside reviews). Verdicts do not change yet: only the passport's and `/verify`'s `issuer_factory` fact is affected, and it is `null` while the mirror rebuilds.
  - **Finalized blocks only.** It scans to the chain's own `finalized` block, never the head, so a creation that a reorganisation drops is never recorded. An RPC that cannot say which block is finalized is an error.
  - **Logs checked before they count.** A log must be emitted by the pinned factory under the pinned topic, not be `removed`, lie inside the range asked, and carry 32-byte block and transaction hashes. The block hash is kept with the token, and `issuer_factory` now shows it, with `finalized: true`.
  - **Success recorded apart from attempts.** `last_ok` (when, which target block, when the scan began) is written only by a run that reached its target; `last_error` and `updated_at` record every attempt. The stale-mirror alert reads `last_ok`, so a failing RPC no longer looks fresh. A first backfill that keeps moving without errors is reported and does not page.
  - **One validator for both readers.** The scanner and the web process both use `validMirror`, which checks the schema, the pins, the block numbers and the times. A file from another factory, topic or start block, or from the old schema, is not read: it is kept once as `mirror.v1.json` and the mirror is rebuilt from the start. On Base the rebuild takes some hours of runs.
  - **Recorded creations are checked again, and can be taken back.** Once a day, oldest first and at most 150 a run, each creation's receipt must still be in the same block, successful, with the factory's log creating that token. One the chain no longer holds moves from `tokens` to `revoked`, with when and why. `issuer_factory` then says `created: false` with `revoked`, the scan never records it again by itself, and `mirror-revoked` alerts for 7 days. After outside review: a creation is revoked only on proof from the block (the recorded block is no longer the chain's block at that height, or it is and holds no factory log creating the token). A missing receipt or transaction alone never revokes, since a node with a pruned index answers null for every older one. A later valid creation log restores a revoked token, and a refused mirror file keeps its revocations through the rebuild.
  - **A missed log cannot hold a listing forever.**
    - An address on the issuer's list that the factory has not been seen creating is noted as waiting. After 30 minutes it is looked for on its own, then again every 6 hours, at most 5 a run.
    - On Base the search bisects for the address's first block with code (historical `eth_getCode`), then reads the factory's log in that one block.
    - On Robinhood Chain, whose RPC keeps no historical state, it reads every factory log again: a dozen calls for the whole chain.
    - A slow reconcile reads the whole range again, a slice per run, and starts over a week after it finishes.
    - Both only add a creation from a log that passes every check; neither removes one.
    - After outside review: the bisection takes only the log for the address it looks for, inside the block it found. It keeps to its time budget at every step and paces its calls. A revoked token still on the list is looked for and restored by a valid log, and a refused mirror file keeps its waiting list.
    - The mirror unit's time limit is now 13 minutes; the timer still runs every 15.
  - **A second RPC, where there is one.** Each creation is asked of a second RPC, run by another operator than the logs gateway, by its receipt.
    - On Base that is Base's own public RPC; on Robinhood Chain none is known, unless the host sets `STP_VERIFY_RPC_ROBINHOOD`.
    - A confirmed creation carries `second_at`.
    - One the second RPC contradicts is revoked only on proof from its own block (the recorded block replaced, or holding no such log). A missing receipt or transaction alone, or an RPC that fails, does neither, and a block that holds the creation confirms it.
    - The mirror records `sources` (1 or 2), and `issuer_factory` shows `rpc_sources` and `second_rpc_at`. The URL is never written down, since a host's own may carry a key.
- **`@proofofstock/guard` 0.3.0 does not take our word for it.** proofofstock.xyz is the one point every verdict passes through, so a "listed" answer is now believed only if:
  - on Base, the address has the form every Coinbase stock token has (`0xb2` and twenty zero hex digits, pinned in the package);
  - by default, the issuer's own list, read by the guard itself and chosen by the chain (never by the answer), has the address.

  A proofofstock.xyz that was broken into can still refuse a buy, but can no longer make one. An address that fails either check is `unknown`, and `failOpen` does not let a contradicted answer through. `crossCheck` is now on by default outside a browser and keeps the issuer's list for a minute, one read per batch. In a browser the issuers' APIs cannot be read (they send no CORS headers), so there it stays off and the prefix check stands alone. New exports: `ISSUER_OF`, `ISSUER_ADDRESS_PREFIX`, `ISSUER_LIST_TTL_MS`, `ISSUER_FAIL_TTL_MS`, `ISSUER_MAX_PAGES`, `readIssuerList`.

  After outside review:
  - An address the issuer's list, read just now, does not have is refused even with `failOpen`, which stays for an issuer that could not be read.
  - A failed read is kept for 15 seconds, so a batch asks the issuer once.
  - Coinbase's list is followed page by page, up to 5.
  - A Web Worker or Service Worker counts as a browser.

### Changed
- The Telegram bot answers in Telegram HTML: the verdict in bold with a mark (listed, look-alike, not listed, unavailable), the chain and its issuer on a line of their own, every contract address as a code span that Telegram copies in one tap, and links to the passport and BaseScan. A ticker answer names the issuer's company beside each contract. Only our own tags (b, i, code, a) are sent and every value is escaped; a token creator's text still never reaches a reply.

### Security
- The public copy gets a plain .gitignore: the patterns that keep a contributor's own secrets, data and build output out of a pull request, with no comment and nothing about our host. The private one, which explains where the keys live and which module is the money path, is no longer exported.
- Paid passes, the money path (owner, 24.11):
  - A v2 payment settled through Coinbase's facilitator is checked on chain before a pass is granted: the receipt must show this payment going to our payee, as for our own settlement. No receipt yet is pending; a receipt that is not this payment grants nothing.
  - The ledgers of passes and payments are kept on disk (`STP_X402_STATE`), so a restart ends no live pass and a settled authorization copied from the chain is "already used", never a second pass. Client addresses are stored as salted hashes; `docs/legal.md` says what is kept and for how long.
  - A settlement that fails after broadcast blocks its payer for 30 minutes, and nobody else; selling as a whole pauses only when ten different payers fail that way within an hour. Before, three failures in a row from anyone paused all sales.
From a self-audit before an outside one (two independent code reviews and a probe of the live service):
- A stale issuer list gives no verdict on any route. Only `/verify` refused a list the issuer had not answered for 15 minutes; the passport, search, the list, the page and the board kept answering from the old copy, so a token delisted while the issuer was unreachable stayed ISSUER-LISTED there. Now every route says it cannot verify.
- An issuer answer that is empty, or that lost more than half the list in one read, is a failed read, not a mass delisting.
- Receipts: the checker requires the address, chain and verdict to be well-formed and to agree, and checks the frozen list's chain, which the archive now names in an `x-pos-chain` header. A rehashed receipt over another chain's list, or one saying `impostor` with `listed: true`, no longer holds. An `impostor` verdict is said to be the service's finding at the time, not something the list re-derives.
- Failed payments are capped per client on every paid route, not only on the pass route: a client over its free minute could otherwise keep the settlement queue busy for real buyers.
- The footer badge filter refuses links under any namespace prefix, unquoted links, backslash escapes, character references and `image-set`.
- The threat model and `SECURITY.md` describe what runs: the facilitator API key and the bot token beside the two keys, that a v2 pass is granted on Coinbase's facilitator's answer, what text of a caller can reach a sentence of ours, the badge from Glama, and the limits not defended against (passes across a restart, breaker griefing, the unmetered badge).
