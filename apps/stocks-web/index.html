Is it the issuer's token?

Coinbase lists its stock tokens on Base and Robinhood lists its own on Robinhood Chain. Only an exact contract address from those liststhat list counts. Beside each list we read the issuer's own token factory on the chain, so a check of an address has a second source. On Base we also read the issuer's transfer rules (B20) from the chain: open, restricted or paused. A token that copies the name or symbol is marked not the issuer's, and you get a warning before any link to it opens.

The issuer's price for one token (Coinbase NAV or Robinhood token bid/ask), always with its age. These prices stop moving while the US market is closed. A price from 40 hours ago is normal on a Sunday and odd on a Tuesday afternoon.

How far has the chain drifted?

Every 15 minutes we compare the deepest on-chain pool with that price and keep the history, split into market open, extended hours and closed. Under 0.5% stays grey. Green: on-chain above the issuer's price. Red: below it. Bold: more than 2%.

Questions

If a token is issuer-listed, is it safe to buy?

Listed means only that this exact contract address is on the issuer's published list for that chain, as we last read it. It says nothing about the price: pools can trade several percent away, mostly while the market is closed, so check the gap and the price's age first. It is not investment advice.

A token is not on the list. Is it fake?

Not necessarily. It may be a token from an issuer we do not cover yet, or an unrelated coin that shares a ticker. We call it an impostor only when it copies a listed token's distinctive symbol or an issuer's naming style.

Where does the data come from?

From each issuer's own public list of its tokens and its price, and from public DEX pool data. Every answer names its source and time, so you can check it there. Proof of Stock is independent: no issuer has reviewed or endorsed this site.

How old can an answer be, and what if a source is down?

Issuer lists are reread every 5 minutes and pool prices recorded every 15; past 30 minutes it is marked stale. The issuer's own price can be hours old while the market is closed, and that age is shown too. If a list cannot be read for more than 15 minutes, every answer says it cannot verify: never "not listed", and never "listed" from an old copy.

Has anyone audited it? Why trust it?

No independent audit has been published, and we do not claim one. What there is instead: every answer can be checked without trusting us (the issuer's list by its hash, the past in Bitcoin, a price on chain at its block), and every day a public exam asks the site about every token whose right answer comes from the issuers themselves and publishes each wrong answer in the exam branch of our public repository, with the hash of the question set so anyone can rerun it. The checks, step by step, are under Proofs at the top of the page. A proved false "listed" earns a reward of 100 USD.

Is it safe to connect my agent?

It never touches your wallet: no sign-in, and no tool that signs or sends a transaction for you. Our own server holds two keys of its own, both holding only gas. One writes our daily anchor hash to the two chains, from its own address, value zero. The other only submits a paid pass's USDC payment exactly as the buyer signed it: it cannot change who is paid or how much. A token's own name is its creator's text: it comes back as a short label, never inside a sentence the service says. One tool reaches outside: simulate_buy asks an aggregator and the chain's node for a live quote and a dry run of one swap and, if you give it a public wallet address, sends that address to them for that one answer. It never signs, never sends and returns no transaction.

Is it free, and can I call it from code?

Yes, with no key and no account. Agents use the MCP server, programs the HTTP API, people the Telegram bot, and each client gets 60 calls in any 60 seconds across the API and MCP: a passport counts as 3 calls, so about 20 passports a minute, and a batch of up to 10 addresses as one. A simulate_buy, which asks for a live quote, counts as 10 (15 with a wallet). An agent that needs more can buy itself a 10-minute pass for 0.01 USDC on Base, still with no account.

Can I check an answer myself, months later?

Yes, and without trusting us. Every answer names the issuer list it was decided against by the hash of its sorted addresses, with a command that computes the same hash from the issuer's own API, and that list stays served by its hash after the issuer has changed it. Ask for receipt=1 and keep what comes back: a small script in our repository rechecks it later. And once a day we timestamp a manifest of the day's data files into Bitcoin.
